Don't Let a Small CAC Mistake Delay Your Business Dreams

We see it all the time — and it's 100% avoidable. Here's what actually causes registration delays, and how to sidestep them.

Delays are rarely about CAC being slow

Most business owners assume registration delays come from the system being slow or bureaucratic. In practice, the majority of delays trace back to small, fixable errors in the application itself — errors that could have been caught before submission.

The delays that catch people out most

A name too close to an existing one. If your proposed name is identical or confusingly similar to a name already on the register, it gets rejected — sending you back to the name-search stage and losing days.
ID details that don't match across documents. A middle name spelled differently on your ID versus your application, or a date of birth mismatch, is enough to trigger a query and pause your file.
Blurry or cropped document uploads. If a reviewer can't clearly read your ID or passport photo, your application sits in a queue waiting on a resubmission request instead of moving forward.
An unclear description of what the business does. Vague or overly broad business objectives sometimes get sent back for clarification — be specific about what your business actually does.

How to avoid these, in practice

Before you submit: Do a name availability search first, and have 2–3 backup names ready in case your first choice isn't available.
Before you upload: Check every document against your typed application, letter for letter — name spelling, ID number, date of birth.
Before you attach a photo/ID scan: Make sure it's well-lit, uncropped, and fully legible — not a screenshot of a screenshot.
Before you write your business description: Be specific. "Sale of provisions and household goods" moves faster than "trading."

The short version

1. Search and reserve your name before anything else.
2. Cross-check every document detail against your application.
3. Upload clear, legible scans — not blurry phone photos.
4. Write a specific, plain description of your business activities.
5. Submit once, correctly — rather than submitting fast and fixing later.
