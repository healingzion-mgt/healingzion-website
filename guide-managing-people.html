Managing Different People: Challenges & Solutions

A practical guide for HZ MGT Resource Hub

Managing a team means managing different personalities, work styles, and expectations — often all at once. Here are the challenges most Nigerian SME owners run into, and practical ways to handle them.

1 The Resistant Employee

Some staff push back on new systems, processes, or instructions — often because change feels like extra work or a threat to how they've always done things.

Solution

Explain the "why" behind a change, not just the "what." People resist less when they understand the reason. Involve them early in small decisions so they feel ownership, not just compliance.

2 The Underperformer

Low output isn't always about attitude — it can be unclear expectations, poor training, or personal issues outside work.

Solution

Have a direct, private conversation focused on specifics ("these 3 tasks were late this month") rather than character judgments. Set a clear, time-bound improvement plan, and follow up consistently.

3 The Overconfident Employee

Skilled but hard to manage — they may talk over others, resist feedback, or bypass processes because "they know better."

Solution

Give them ownership of a clear area of responsibility so their confidence has a productive outlet. Set firm boundaries on process (deadlines, reporting) that apply to everyone, no exceptions.

4 The Quiet or Withdrawn Employee

They may have good ideas but rarely speak up in meetings, making it hard to know what they're really thinking.

Solution

Create 1:1 check-ins instead of relying only on group settings. Ask specific, open-ended questions ("what's one thing slowing you down this week?") rather than generic ones.

5 Personality Clashes Between Team Members

Two capable employees may simply not work well together — different communication styles, pace, or values.

Solution

Don't ignore it hoping it resolves itself. Mediate early with both people in the room, focus on the specific behavior/incident (not personality), and agree on how they'll handle disagreements going forward.

6 Generational / Experience Gaps

Younger staff and long-time employees can clash over technology, formality, or ways of working.

Solution

Pair people across the gap for specific projects — younger staff bring new tools/ideas, experienced staff bring institutional knowledge. Frame it as mutual learning, not one side "teaching" the other.

The Common Thread

Most people-management problems come down to unclear expectations and infrequent feedback. Regular, specific, private conversations solve more problems than any policy document.
