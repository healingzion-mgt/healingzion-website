Registering Your Business With CAC

Most people lose time and money on avoidable mistakes. Here's how to register the right way, the first time.

Have you registered your business?

If not: registration is what makes your business a legal entity — able to open a corporate bank account, sign contracts in its own name, apply for loans or grants, and be taken seriously by bigger clients and partners.

If you already have: registration only makes your business legal — it doesn't make it grow. Legal status is the foundation, not the strategy. The rest of this guide covers both getting registered correctly and what to do once you are.

3 mistakes people make registering with CAC

1. Using a name that's already taken. Many applications get rejected or delayed simply because the proposed business name is too similar to an existing registered name. Always run a name availability search on the CAC portal before you get attached to a name.
2. Skipping proper document verification. Incomplete or mismatched documents (ID numbers that don't match, unclear passport photos, inconsistent addresses) are one of the most common causes of delay. Double-check every document against your application before submitting.
3. Missing post-registration compliance steps. Getting your certificate is the beginning, not the end. Many business owners register and then never file annual returns, which quietly builds up penalties over time (see below).

Avoid the stress — get it right the first time rather than fixing it after a rejection or a fine.

What you'll need before you start

  • 2–3 proposed business names, in order of preference
  • A valid means of ID (National ID, international passport, or driver's license)
  • Passport photograph
  • Proof of address for the business (utility bill or similar)
  • Details of the owner(s)/directors — full names, addresses, phone numbers, emails
  • A brief description of what the business does (its objectives)

The registration process, simplified

Step 1 — Name search & reservation: Search your preferred name on the CAC portal. If available, reserve it (this typically holds it for a limited window while you complete the rest).
Step 2 — Choose your business structure: Business name (simplest, for sole proprietors), or a limited liability company (separates your personal assets from the business, better for growth and credibility).
Step 3 — Complete the application: Fill in the required forms with owner/director details, upload your documents, and pay the applicable fee.
Step 4 — Await approval: Processing times vary, but incomplete or inconsistent documentation is the main thing that slows this down — see the mistakes above.
Step 5 — Receive your certificate: Once approved, you'll receive your registration certificate and a unique registration number — keep both safe, you'll need them for banking, contracts, and compliance filings.

Don't stop at the certificate

Registering only makes your business legal — it doesn't make it visible or profitable. Once you're registered:

  • Open a dedicated business bank account using your certificate
  • Keep basic financial records from day one, not once things get complicated
  • File your annual returns with CAC every year, even if nothing changed
  • Start thinking about growth — marketing, systems, and the right contacts — registration is the foundation, not the finish line

The short version — what to do this week

1. Run a name search on the CAC portal before you fall in love with a name.
2. Gather your documents and double-check every detail matches.
3. Decide: business name or limited liability company, based on your growth plans.
4. Submit your application and keep your certificate and registration number safe once approved.
5. Open a business account and put annual returns on a recurring reminder.
