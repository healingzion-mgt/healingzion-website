Have You Registered Your Business?

Wherever you are in the journey, there's a next right step. Read the path that matches where you are.

If you haven't registered yet

If no

Registration is what turns your business from an idea or side hustle into a legal entity — able to open a corporate bank account, sign contracts in its own name, apply for loans or grants, and be taken seriously by bigger clients and partners.

You don't need everything figured out to start. You need: a couple of backup names, a valid ID, a real address, and a clear sense of what your business does. From there, the process is a straightforward sequence — name search, application, document upload, payment, review, certificate.

The businesses that struggle most later are usually the ones that delayed registering while actively trading — because by the time they do register, they're untangling contracts, bank accounts, and relationships that were built informally.

If you've already registered

If yes

Registering only makes your business legal — it doesn't make it grow, and it doesn't make it visible. That part is on you, from here.

A few things worth checking now that you're registered:

Are you filing annual returns? This is separate from tax filing, and it's the one most business owners forget entirely — until the penalties or a struck-off notice catch them off guard.

Do you have a dedicated business bank account? Mixing personal and business money makes it much harder to track what the business is actually earning, and undermines the liability protection a company structure is supposed to give you.

Are you keeping basic financial records? Not elaborate books — just enough to know your numbers and be ready if a bank, investor, or the CAC ever asks.

Beyond compliance, growth comes down to the same fundamentals for every business: clear positioning, consistent visibility to the right customers, and systems that don't depend entirely on you being present every day.

The short version

1. Not registered yet? Start with a name search — that's the only real first step.
2. Already registered? Confirm your annual returns are up to date.
3. Either way: separate business and personal finances as early as possible.
4. Registration is the foundation. Visibility, systems, and consistency are what build on top of it.
